# Levelling

---

Match each term with its correct definition.
<DragAndDrop words="automatic level;digital level;dumpy level;tilting level;circular bubble;compensator;crosshairs;objective lens;parallax;reticle;tribrach">

<DropZone answer="dumpy level"/> A.  An older type of level whose telescope is rigidly fixed to the vertical spindle, so the whole body must be carefully levelled with footscrews before every single reading.
<DropZone answer="tilting level"/> B.  A level with a separate fine-levelling screw and a small split bubble that the observer must centre immediately before each individual reading is taken.
<DropZone answer="digital level"/> C.  An electronic instrument that automatically reads a specially marked staff and displays the height difference on screen, removing the risk of a misread value.
<DropZone answer="compensator"/> D.  An internal mechanism, often a suspended prism, that automatically keeps the line of sight horizontal once the instrument has been roughly levelled.
<DropZone answer="circular bubble"/> E.  A small round spirit level used to level an instrument quickly and approximately, before any fine adjustment is carried out.
<DropZone answer="tribrach"/> F.  A detachable base fitted with three footscrews, allowing an instrument to be levelled and exchanged without re-levelling the tripod itself.
<DropZone answer="crosshairs/reticle"/>, <DropZone answer="crosshairs/reticle"/> G.  Fine lines etched on glass inside the telescope and used to align the line of sight precisely on the staff.
<DropZone answer="parallax"/> H.  The apparent movement of the crosshairs against the staff image, caused by incorrect focusing of the eyepiece or objective lens.
<DropZone answer="objective lens"/> I.  The lens at the front (far) end of the telescope, which gathers light and forms the image of the staff.
<DropZone answer="automatic level"/> J.  A modern piece of equipment containing a compensator, so that only rough levelling with the circular bubble is required before each sight is automatically corrected.
</DragAndDrop>
