Match each term with its definition.

<DragAndDrop words="datum;benchmark;geoid;reference ellipsoid;orthometric height;misclosure;reduced level;datum transformation;tide gauge;subsidence;control network;geoid model">

<DropZone answer="subsidence"/>  the gradual sinking of the ground surface, which can make published heights unreliable

<DropZone answer="reference ellipsoid"/>  a mathematically defined surface, produced by rotating an ellipse about its minor axis, used to approximate the shape of the Earth

<DropZone answer="geoid model"/>  a gridded set of values that allows ellipsoidal heights from GNSS to be converted into heights above a vertical datum

<DropZone answer="1benchmark"/>  a permanent, clearly marked point whose height above a vertical datum has been accurately determined and published

<DropZone answer="datum transformation"/>  the mathematical process of converting coordinates from one reference system into another

<DropZone answer="reduced level"/>  the height of a point calculated from staff readings and related to the chosen datum

<DropZone answer="geoid"/>  the equipotential surface of the Earth's gravity field that best approximates global mean sea level

<DropZone answer="control network"/> a framework of accurately coordinated points on which further surveys are based

<DropZone answer="datum"/>  a reference surface or point, defined by agreed parameters, from which positions or heights are measured

<DropZone answer="misclosure"/>  the discrepancy between the observed and the known value when a levelling line closes on itself or on another known mark

<DropZone answer="orthometric height"/> the height of a point above the geoid, measured along the plumb line

<DropZone answer="tide gauge"/>  an instrument that records the rise and fall of sea level at a fixed location over time
