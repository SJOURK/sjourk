Land and Building Register (EGiB), also known as the real estate cadastre, a public register of numerical and descriptive data concerning land, buildings and premises, as well as data on the owners of real estate (or, where ownership data is unavailable, data on the people and organizational units that control such real estate), as defined in the regulation of the Minister of Development, Labour and Technology on the land and building register. The regulation specifies, among other things:
·the method of establishing the land and building register
·the method of maintaining the register and detailed rules for the exchange of registry data
·the detailed scope of information covered by the register, as well as the scope of information covered by the property price register 
In Poland, the land and <Blank answer="building"/> register is legally equated with the  <Blank answer="real estate"/> cadastre.
The land and building register is established and maintained within an IT system, based on computerized registry databases. These computerized registry databases form an integral part of the National Geodetic and Cartographic Resource. The register covers the entire territory of the Republic of Poland, with the exception of territorial waters. For registry purposes, the country is divided into the following spatial units:
·registry unit – an area of land located within the administrative boundaries of a gmina (commune), and, where the gmina includes a locality with town/city status, also within the administrative boundaries of that town/city. In cities where districts have been created as auxiliary units of the gmina, a registry unit may consist of the area of one or several districts. A registry unit is divided into registry precincts.
·registry precinct – an area of land located within the boundaries of a village or a sołectwo (village administrative unit), or, in cities, within the boundaries of districts, housing estates and urban complexes, or an area delimited by natural boundaries such as watercourses, streets, railway lines and other physiographic features
·registry plot (parcel) – a continuous area of land located within the boundaries of a single precinct, legally homogeneous, and separated from its surroundings by boundary lines. Plots located within the boundaries of a single precinct that form part of a single property together constitute a land register unit.
The land and building register consists of computerized registry databases and registry files. Based on the registry databases, reports illustrating registry data are generated for individual precincts:
1.land register
2.building register
3.premises register
4.building record card
5.premises record card
6.registry map
The data contained in the register are subject to updating and are made available to the public. The register does not include buildings whose construction, under building-law regulations, does not require a permit or notification.
The land and building register is maintained by starostas (county heads) or by the presidents of cities with county rights. In practice, as noted by the Supreme Audit Office (NIK), the register is often out of date, maintained in a chaotic manner, and local governments routinely violate regulations on the de-agriculturalization (change of land-use classification) of land.
