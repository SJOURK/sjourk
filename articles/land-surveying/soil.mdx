# Soil

---

Match the terms to their definitions.

<DragAndDrop words="bearing capacity;bedrock(R horizon);illuviation;horizon;leaching(eluviation);in situ;parent material(C horizon);soil profile;subsoil(B horizon);topsoil(A horizon);trial pit;water table;weathering">

<DropZone answer="soil profile"/> A vertical cross-section through the ground, from the surface down to bedrock, showing the sequence of distinct layers that have developed over time.

<DropZone answer="horizon"/> A layer within a soil profile, roughly parallel to the surface, that differs from the layers above and below it in colour, texture, structure and composition.

<DropZone answer="topsoil(A horizon)"/> The uppermost mineral layer, enriched with decomposed organic matter (humus) and typically the most biologically active part of the profile.

<DropZone answer="subsoil(B horizon)"/> The layer beneath the topsoil in which minerals and fine particles carried down from above tend to accumulate.

<DropZone answer="parent material(C horizon)"/> The partly weathered rock or sediment, often called regolith, from which the overlying soil has gradually developed.

<DropZone answer="bedrock(R horizon)"/> The solid, largely unweathered rock that underlies the soil profile and marks its lower boundary.

<DropZone answer="leaching(eluviation)"/> The removal of soluble substances and fine particles from an upper horizon as water percolates downward through it.

<DropZone answer="illuviation"/> The accumulation, in a lower horizon, of material that has been washed down and deposited from the horizons above.

<DropZone answer="weathering"/> The physical and chemical breakdown of rock into progressively smaller fragments, which ultimately supplies the mineral component of soil.

<DropZone answer="water table"/> The upper surface of the zone in which the ground is permanently saturated with groundwater.

<DropZone answer="trial pit"/> A pit excavated during a ground investigation to allow direct, in situ examination and logging of the soil profile.

<DropZone answer="bearing capacity"/> The maximum load a soil can safely support without undergoing excessive settlement or shear failure.

<DropZone answer="in situ"/> In its original, undisturbed location, rather than after removal to a laboratory.
