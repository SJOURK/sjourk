Match each term to its definition.

<DragAndDrop words="geomatics;geoinformatics;geoinformation;GNSS;LiDAR;georeferencing;spatial data infrastructure;interoperability;point cloud;metadata;remote sensing;digital elevation model">
<DropZone answer="geomatics"/> A broad, interdisciplinary profession that combines surveying, cartography, remote sensing, GPS and GIS to gather, manage and interpret spatial data.
<DropZone answer="geoinformatics"/> The technology and applied science of designing computational methods, algorithms and systems for capturing, storing, processing and disseminating geographic data.
<DropZone answer="geoinformation"/> Meaningful knowledge obtained once raw spatial data has been processed, analysed and interpreted, ready to inform planning or decision-making.
<DropZone answer="remote sensing"/> The acquisition of data about the Earth's surface from a distance, usually by means of sensors mounted on satellites or aircraft.
<DropZone answer="georeferencing"/> The process of linking a dataset to a known real-world coordinate system so that it can be correctly overlaid with other geographic layers.
<DropZone answer="spatial data infrastructure"/> A coordinated set of standards, technologies, policies and institutional agreements that enables geospatial data to be discovered, accessed and shared efficiently.
<DropZone answer="digital elevation model"/> A raster or vector dataset representing the bare-earth surface, with vegetation, buildings and other man-made structures digitally removed.
<DropZone answer="GNSS"/> A satellite-based system that provides positioning, navigation and timing information anywhere on or near the Earth's surface.
<DropZone answer="LiDAR"/> A remote-sensing technique that uses pulsed laser light to measure distances and generate highly accurate three-dimensional representations of a surface.
<DropZone answer="interoperability"/> The ability of different systems, software or organisations to exchange and make use of spatial data without additional conversion effort.
<DropZone answer="point cloud"/> A large set of individual measured points, typically produced by a laser scanner, that together represent the three-dimensional shape of an object or surface.
<DropZone answer="metadata"/> "Data about data": structured information describing the source, accuracy, date and format of a spatial dataset.

</DragAndDrop>
