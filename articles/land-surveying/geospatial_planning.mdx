# Geospatial Planning

---

Match each term to its definition.

<DragAndDrop words="brownfield site;densification;environmental impact assessment;gentrification;greenfield site;land-use planning;mixed-use development;stakeholder engagement;territorial cohesion;urban sprawl;urban regeneration;zoning">

<DropZone answer="zoning"/> A.  The regulatory division of an area into zones, within each of which particular uses of land are permitted or prohibited.
<DropZone answer="urban sprawl"/> B.  The uncontrolled, low-density expansion of a city into surrounding rural or undeveloped land, typically reliant on private cars.
<DropZone answer="brownfield site"/> C.  A previously developed piece of land, often industrial, that is now vacant or underused and may require decontamination before redevelopment.
<DropZone answer="greenfield site"/> D.  An undeveloped piece of land, usually agricultural or on the edge of a settlement, that has never previously been built on.
<DropZone answer="urban regeneration"/> E.  The renewal of a run-down urban area through investment in infrastructure, housing and public space, often intended to attract new residents and businesses.
<DropZone answer="gentrification"/> F.  A process by which an original, often lower-income, urban neighbourhood is transformed by the arrival of wealthier residents and businesses, frequently displacing existing residents.
<DropZone answer="land-use planning"/> G.  The public-sector process of determining the future use of land and buildings, and coordinating the location and timing of development.
<DropZone answer="stakeholder engagement"/> H.  The practice of involving residents, businesses and other interested parties in the planning process so that their views inform the final decision.
<DropZone answer="environmental impact assessment"/> I.  A systematic process used to identify, predict and evaluate the likely environmental consequences of a proposed project before a decision is made.
<DropZone answer="mixed-use development"/> J.  A type of development that combines residential, commercial and sometimes industrial uses within a single site or building, reducing the need to travel.
<DropZone answer="densification"/> K.  The process of increasing the number of people, dwellings or jobs within a given urban area, often through taller buildings or the subdivision of existing plots.
<DropZone answer="territorial cohesion"/> L.  A policy goal aimed at reducing economic, social and territorial disparities between regions so that all areas can develop harmoniously.
