# Surveying Instruments 

---

Match the names of surveying instruments to their definitions.

<DragAndDrop words="Auto level;Chain;Compass;Digital level;Dumpy level;EDM (Electronic Distance Meter);Field book;GNSS/GPS receiver;Laser level;Levelling staff;Measuring tape;Plane table;Planimeter;Plumb bob;Prismatic compass;Ranging rods;Surveying prism;Theodolite;Total station;Tripod">

<DropZone answer="chain"/>  A traditional measuring device made of linked metal segments, once widely used to measure horizontal distances between survey points.

<DropZone answer="measuring tape"/> A flexible, graduated tape used to obtain accurate short-distance measurements on site.

<DropZone answer="compass"/> An instrument that uses a magnetised needle to establish direction and determine bearings relative to magnetic north.

<DropZone answer="plane table"/> A drawing board mounted on a tripod that allows surveyors to plot map details directly in the field as they are observed.

<DropZone answer="prismatic compass"/> A refined compass fitted with a sighting vane and a small prism, allowing the user to read a magnetic bearing while sighting the target.

<DropZone answer="dumpy level"/> A traditional optical instrument with a fixed telescope, used to determine differences in elevation between points.

<DropZone answer="auto level"/> A levelling instrument fitted with an internal compensator that automatically maintains a horizontal line of sight once roughly set up.

<DropZone answer="digital level"/> An electronic levelling instrument that reads a bar-coded staff automatically and displays the elevation on screen, reducing human reading error.

<DropZone answer="theodolite"/> A precision instrument mounted on a tripod that measures both horizontal and vertical angles for triangulation and angular control.

<DropZone answer="total station"/> An integrated electronic instrument that combines angle measurement, distance measurement and automatic data recording in a single unit.

<DropZone answer="GNSS/GPS receiver"/> A satellite-based receiver that determines precise geographic coordinates at any point on the earth's surface.

<DropZone answer="EDM (Electronic Distance Meter)"/> A device that measures distance electronically, by timing a signal that travels to a target and is reflected back.

<DropZone answer="laser level"/> An instrument that projects a visible horizontal or vertical laser line, used as a reference for levelling or alignment.

<DropZone answer="levelling staff"/> A graduated rod, read through a level's telescope, used together with the instrument to find differences in elevation.

<DropZone answer="ranging rods"/> Straight, brightly banded poles planted at survey points to mark their position and help establish a straight line of sight.

<DropZone answer="plumb bob"/> A pointed weight suspended on a string, used to establish a true vertical line over a survey point.

<DropZone answer="tripod"/> A three-legged stand that provides a stable, adjustable base for instruments such as levels, theodolites and total stations.

<DropZone answer="surveying prism"/> A retro-reflective target mounted on a pole that reflects an EDM's or total station's signal directly back to the instrument.

<DropZone answer="field book"/> A notebook used on site to record measurements, sketches and observations as a permanent record of the survey.

<DropZone answer="planimetre"/> A mechanical or digital instrument that measures the area of an irregular shape by tracing its outline on a map or plan.
